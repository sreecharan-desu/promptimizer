---

Savings without a quality score are marketing. Promptimizer measures both.

## Two checks

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Online guard">
    After a cheap completion, if the answer is empty, shorter than 80 characters on L4+, or contains a refusal (`I don't know`, `too complex`, `as a small model`), we escalate and retry. The extra cost is real. The quality is preserved.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Benchmark score">
    Each of the 15 gold tasks is scored with required-concept coverage + structure. The dashboard compares four policies and prints average quality, worst-case quality, and the delta vs always-frontier.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## What “degraded” means

- Missing required concepts (`token bucket`, `Euclid`, `peeking`…)
- A thin outline on a system-design prompt
- An explicit small-model refusal

Economy answers on L1 factual tasks are allowed to be short. That is not degradation.

## How to fail on purpose

Route everything to nano. `saved_pct` looks heroic. `avg_quality_routed` collapses on the five hard rows. That is the slide you do **not** want.

## Threshold

`QUALITY_ESCALATE_THRESHOLD` (default `0.62`). Below that, the row is a quality fail even if we did not escalate.
